Household tasks, together — fairly

Torchons & Serviettes helps a household — couple, flat-share, family — share the chores so the same person isn't always the one thinking about them. Recurrence, priorities, fair share, personal preferences.

No traditional sign-up: no e-mail, no password. No ads, no tracking, no audience measurement tool.

What the app does

  • Recurring tasksTasks that come back (every X days, more often in summer, on a fixed date…) shared across the household.
    Learn more

    Five patterns: "Regular", every X days; "Varies by month", a different frequency per month, for whatever follows the seasons; "On a fixed date" on the calendar, with no drift even if you do it late; "Anniversary of the first time", which comes back on the anniversary of the first completion; or "Irregular", whenever it is needed — the app then learns the real cadence. One-shot tasks exist too ("Once only"): once done, they archive themselves.

  • Urgency at a glanceGreen / amber / red: see at once what is pressing and what can wait.
    Learn more

    The colour comes from a computed due date rather than a feeling: last done plus interval. Amber lights up in proportion to the frequency — three days ahead for a monthly task, the day before for a weekly one.

  • Fair shareA ratio you set per member: the load can be fair, not merely equal.
    Learn more

    Not everyone can do as much, and 50/50 is not always fair. You set the target ratio, the app tracks the real gap over the last 30 days — a rolling window, so a bad month does not follow you around.

  • Everyone's preferencesOne prefers vacuuming, the other the dishes: the app takes that into account.
    Learn more

    Each person rates what they like doing and what they avoid. When the app suggests the next task, it offers it first to whoever minds least — without ever letting anyone off the hook.

  • EffortEvery task carries an effort: 5 minutes of dishes is not a deep clean, and the maths knows it.
    Learn more

    Effort is voted on by the members, not decreed by the app: you are the one who knows what cleaning your oven costs. It weights the fair share — ten easy tasks do not equal one slog.

  • Handling absencesEveryone away for two weeks? The app will not ask you to vacuum on the way back in.
    Learn more

    Days when the household is empty do not count against tasks that require being there. Detection is automatic via home WiFi, and you can always correct a day by hand.

  • Catching upHow many minutes a day to get back to zero, and how long it will take.
    Learn more

    The backlog becomes a number rather than a worry. The app compares your real pace over the last fortnight with the pace needed, and lets you simulate: what if I did 20 minutes a day?

  • Motivation, if you want itPoints, badges and streaks — or none of it. A choice, not a fixture.
    Learn more

    Half of households want none of it, and that is fine: gamification turns off with one switch, for the whole household or for you alone. Nothing is lost if you turn it on later — the points counted quietly show up all at once.

  • The app grows with youOn install: tasks and colours. The rest turns up when it becomes useful.
    Learn more

    None of what is described here shows up on day one. The app offers it when something makes it relevant — someone joins the household, you come back from a fortnight away, one task keeps drifting. One proposal a week at most, and never twice if you said no.

  • OfflineWorks with no network and syncs on its own as soon as the connection returns.
    Learn more

    Everything stays readable and editable with no network: the cellar, the underground, the house in the country. Changes queue up and reconcile when the network returns, without overwriting what the other person did meanwhile.
