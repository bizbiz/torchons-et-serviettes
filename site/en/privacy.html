Privacy policy

Last updated: November 16, 2026

Torchons & Serviettes is designed to collect the bare minimum. No e-mail, no password, no phone number, no geographic position, no ads, no tracking, no audience measurement tool.

This English version is a translation provided for convenience. In case of discrepancy, the French version prevails as the legally binding text.

In short, in plain words
  • We don't ask for your e-mail, a password or your phone number.
  • Your nickname, the tasks and what you have done are shared with the members of your household, and kept on our server, in France.
  • The app does not collect your position. If you turn it on, it only reads the name of your Wi-Fi, to know whether you are at home.
  • Under 15? Ask a parent for permission before using the app.
  • You can delete your account in the app whenever you want. To erase everything right away, write to us.

1. Data controller

The data controller is Quentin BIZOUARD, a natural person publishing the Torchons & Serviettes app on an individual basis (France). Contact: contact@torchonsetserviettes.app.

2. Data collected, purposes and legal basis

Sign-up does not use a traditional account: no e-mail or password is requested. The data below is the only data the app's server processes.

Legal basis. The data needed for the service you use (nickname, household, tasks, completions, preferences and votes, devices, notifications, and the Wi-Fi name if you turn on presence detection) is processed to perform that service (GDPR article 6(1)(b)). The server's technical logs and request rate limiting rely on the legitimate interest in protecting the service against outages and abuse (article 6(1)(f), recital 49). The history kept under a pseudonym after you leave relies on the other household members' legitimate interest in keeping their statistics fair (section 6).

Three exchanges with Google do not go through our server. When the app is offline and you open its connection diagnosis, it tests the Internet by calling connectivitycheck.gstatic.com (Google then sees the phone's IP address; nothing else is sent). To join a household, you can scan the invitation QR code: the app then uses the camera ("camera" permission) and Google's ML Kit code reader, whose model is built into the app. According to Google's documentation, ML Kit sends Google technical information: phone manufacturer and model, Android version, app name and version, a per-installation identifier, performance metrics, the size of the processed images and error codes; the images themselves are not on that list. Finally, the app embeds the Google Play Billing component; it is prepared for a possible paid option, which does not exist today: no purchase is offered and no payment data is collected.

Not collected: e-mail (unless you write to us), password, phone number, photos (the camera is only used to read a QR code; no image is sent to our server), contacts, banking or payment data, and geographic position: Android files reading the Wi-Fi name under the "location" permission, which is why the app asks for it, but it neither reads nor sends any coordinates (neither GPS nor mobile network). This permission is optional: without it, you confirm your presence by hand.

3. No tracking, no cookies

The app embeds no audience analytics tool, no third-party crash reporter, no advertising tracker. The only technical exception: the diagnostic information that the ML Kit QR code reader itself sends to Google (section 2); we have no access to it. This website sets no cookies and loads no external resources (fonts, scripts, CDN).

Direct consequence of this choice: no bug is reported automatically. Fixing issues therefore relies on user reports — by e-mail to contact@torchonsetserviettes.app or via the public repository's issues (link added on opening). See the Contact page.

5. Transfers outside the European Union

The app's data is hosted in France (Scaleway). Three flows leave the European Union for US companies: push notifications (Google Firebase Cloud Messaging), the QR code reader's diagnostic information (Google ML Kit), and this website as well as e-mails sent to the contact address (Cloudflare, then the Google Gmail inbox that receives them). These transfers rely on the European Commission's adequacy decision of July 10, 2023 (EU–US Data Privacy Framework), and failing that on the Commission's Standard Contractual Clauses provided for in these providers' terms. The Android backup and the connectivity test described in section 2 are Google's and follow its own policies.

What Google sees in a notification. Visible notifications are passed in clear to Google, which delivers them. Their text contains the pseudonym of the member concerned and the task name (for example "Alex did: Vacuum"), sometimes the date of the completion. For a re-vote, it also contains the task's room(s) or category(ies), the kind of vote and, in the answer, its value (for example "from 15 to 10 min"). Other, invisible messages keep the app up to date live: they carry technical identifiers (of a proposal, a task or a member) and, for the answer to a re-vote, the pseudonym of whoever answered and the vote values.

6. Retention and deletion

You can delete your account directly from the app (Settings › Delete my account). Leaving your last household has the same effects, without the account being marked deleted. Here is what happens, as the server does it:

  • Immediately: you leave all your households, the account is marked deleted, and every phone connected to the account is signed out and erases its local data on its next call to the server. The Wi-Fi names you had saved are erased (this happens every time you leave a household). You are removed from shared tasks; your solo tasks are paused.
  • For 30 days: your recovery code ("Recover my household") lets you come back and restore everything.
  • After 30 days (nightly job): the account is pseudonymized, not removed from the database. This is not anonymization: what remains is tied to a technical identifier, and in a small household the other members know who "Former member" was. No return is possible any more.
    • Deleted: your notification tokens, your devices, your settings, your streaks, the feature suggestions made to you, your fair-share settings, the suggestions you had dismissed, your per-task preferences, your badges, the guides you had seen, your objective proposals (with the other members' answers to them), your answers to other members' proposals, your solo tasks (except those another member also completed, kept and still invisible to the others), and the history of the changes you made (if the very last change recorded by the server is yours, it stays, detached from your account, for phone sync).
    • Emptied: the notes of your completions, your minutes-per-day objective (its display unit goes back to the default), and in your account: the display name (replaced by "Former member"), the username (replaced by a technical identifier), the avatar, the recovery code and the last household used.
    • Kept under the name "Former member", as long as the household exists: your completions (date, points), your effort and frequency votes, your role and your dates of arrival and departure in the household, the fair-share history, and mentions of you in the history written by other members. Reason: without them, the other members' statistics and task sharing would be wrong.
  • Households: a household with no active member left is archived (when the last member leaves, or at the latest by the next nightly job), then permanently deleted 365 days later, with its tasks, history, presence calendar and whatever was kept there under a pseudonym. The last trace of your account (its technical identifier and dates) disappears when the last household you belonged to is deleted. As long as a household carries on with other members, what is kept there under a pseudonym stays there.
  • Full erasure on request: if you want everything erased right away, including what is kept under a pseudonym, write to contact@torchonsetserviettes.app. The other household members' statistics will change as a result.

The server's technical logs sometimes contain your account's technical identifier. Deleting the account does not erase them: they disappear on their own through rotation, in about two weeks at current traffic (section 2).

7. Your rights

Under the GDPR, you have the rights of access, rectification, erasure, restriction, objection and portability regarding your data.

  • Erasure: from the app (Settings › Delete my account), with the effects described in section 6; or full and immediate, on request by e-mail.
  • Access and a copy of your data (portability): on request by e-mail.
  • Objection: you can object to the processing based on legitimate interest, that is the technical logs and keeping your history under a pseudonym after you leave.
  • How: write to contact@torchonsetserviettes.app. You get an answer within one month. If the request is complex, this period may be extended by two months; you are then told so within the first month.

You may also lodge a complaint with the French data protection authority CNIL (www.cnil.fr) if you believe the processing is not compliant.

8. Security

No password is stored (the app does not use one). The recovery code is kept only as an argon2id hash, never in clear. Communications between the app and the server are encrypted (HTTPS/TLS).

9. Minors

The app is open to minors: a household is often a family.

  • Under 15? Ask one of your parents (or the person with parental authority) for permission before creating or joining a household.
  • The app does not ask your age and does not check it: checking it would mean collecting more data about you.
  • A parent can exercise their child's rights (section 7) by writing to contact@torchonsetserviettes.app.

10. Changes

This policy may evolve (new feature, change of processor). In case of a material change, the "last updated" date at the top of the page is refreshed. Major changes may be flagged in the app, which links to this page when a household is created and in its Settings.

11. Contact

For any question about this policy or your data: contact@torchonsetserviettes.app.

12. Your connected devices

You can see at any time which devices hold a session on your account, and cut off access for any of them: Settings › Household › My devices. A cut-off device is signed out and its local data is erased the next time it connects. A device that never reconnects keeps what it already holds in memory: no app can erase a phone that stays offline, and we would rather say so than let you assume otherwise.
